Example 18.2.0.4.

In Section 8.7 we analyzed several IVPs of the form
\begin{align} \dfdx{y}{t} = Ky\amp{}\amp{}y(0) = Y_0.\tag{18.15} \end{align}
But in that section our efforts were based on guessing the form of the solution. Since the differential equation in equation (18.15) is separable we no longer need to guess.
🔗
To keep things simple for now we’ll take \(K=1\) and \(Y_0=1\text{.}\)
🔗
First we separate the variables
\begin{equation*} \frac{\dx{y}}{y}=\dx{t}\text{,} \end{equation*}
then we integrate both sides, giving
\begin{equation*} \ln\abs{y} = t + C\text{.} \end{equation*}
Solving for \(\abs{y}\) next we get
\begin{equation*} \abs{y} = e^{t+C} = e^Ce^t\text{.} \end{equation*}
From the initial condition \(y(0) = 1 \) we see that \(e^C=\text{,}\) so
\begin{equation*} \abs{y} = e^t\text{.} \end{equation*}
this is a model of population growth and populations are never negative so the absolute value bars are superfluous and we arrive at
\begin{equation*} y(t) = Ke^t\text{,} \end{equation*}
which is consistent with our results in Section 8.7.
🔗
Finally, from the initial condition \(y(0) =1 \) we see that \(K=1\text{,}\) so
\begin{equation*} y(t) = e^t\text{.} \end{equation*}
