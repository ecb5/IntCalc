Cornerstone Problem 19.1.3.7. Quadratic Drag and Terminal Velocity: Sand Falling Through Air.

An example of quadratic drag occurs when a ball of mass \(M\) falls through the Earth’s atmosphere. In this case buoyancy is typically negligible so we will disregard it.
🔗
First, let \(y(t)\) be distance the ball has fallen (so the positive \(y\) axis is pointing downward), with \(y(0)=0\) representing the initial position of the ball. As always we denote the acceleration due to gravity by \(g\text{,}\) so the weight of the ball is \(Mg\text{.}\) Quadratic drag is proportional to the square of the velocity, so \(-Rv^2\text{,}\) where \(R\) is a constant that will depend on the medium our ball is falling through (air, in this instance) and \(v=\dfdx{v}{t}\) is the velocity. For simplicity, we will assume \(v(0)=0\text{.}\)
🔗

(a)

Use Newton’s Second Law of Motion: (\(\text{force}=\text{mass}\times{}\text{acceleration}\)), to show that the velocity of the ball must satisfy the IVP:
\begin{align} M\dfdx{v}{t}\amp{}=Mg-Rv^2,\amp{} v(0)\amp{}=0 \tag{19.10} \end{align}
🔗
🔗

(b)

Show that the IVP in part (b) is equivalent to
\begin{align} \frac{\dx{v}}{1-\frac{R}{Mg}v^2}=g\dx{t}, \amp{}\amp{} v(0)=0.\tag{19.11} \end{align}
🔗
🔗

(c)

Use the substitution \(\sqrt{\frac{R}{Mg}}v = \sin(\theta)\) to show that the differential equation in IVP (19.11) can be re-expressed as
\begin{equation} \int\sec\left(\theta \right)\dx{\theta } = \sqrt{\frac{Rg}{M}}t+C.\tag{19.12} \end{equation}
🔗
🔗

(d)

Confirm by differentiation that \(\sec\left(\theta \right)\tan\left(\theta \right)\) is an antiderivative of \(\sec\left(\theta \right)\text{.}\) (In Cornerstone Problem 19.1.3.8 we will learn a method for computing \(\int\sec\left(\theta \right)\dx{\theta }\) directly.)
🔗
🔗

(e)

Use the result of part (d) to integrate the left side of equation (19.12) and unwind the substitution \(\sqrt{\frac{R}{Mg}}v=\sin (\theta )\) (from part (c)) to show that
\begin{equation} \ln\sqrt{\frac{1+\sqrt{\frac{R}{Mg}}v}{1-\sqrt{\frac{R}{Mg}}v}} = \sqrt{\frac{Rg}{M}}t +C.\tag{19.13} \end{equation}
🔗
🔗

(f)

Use the initial condition \(v(0)=0 \) to show that \(C=1\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

(g)

Exponentiate both sides of equation (19.13) and solve for \(v\) to show that
\begin{equation*} v=\sqrt{\frac{Mg}{R}}\left[\frac{1-e^{-2\sqrt{\frac{Rg}{M}}t}}{1+e^{-2\sqrt{\frac{Rg}{M}}t}}\right] \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

(h)

Compute \(\limit{t}{\infty}{v}\) to obtain the terminal velocity. Compare this with the terminal velocity we found in Cornerstone Problem: Linear Drag and Terminal Velocity: Sand Falling in Water.
