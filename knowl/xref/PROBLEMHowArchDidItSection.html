Problem 20.1.1.5. How Archimedes Did It.

Aside: Note to Self.

This problem probably belongs in the section on infinite series, not here.
🔗

(a)

Use your result from part (a) of Problem 21.3.0.5 to show that
\begin{equation*} \text{area of }\triangle ACF + \text{area of }\triangle CBE =\frac{1}{4}\text{(area of } \triangle ABC) \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

(b)

Compute the sum of the areas of the triangles constructed using the mid-points of \(\overline{AB}\text{,}\) \(\overline{FC}\text{,}\) \(\overline{CE}\text{,}\) and \(\overline{EB}\text{.}\) Then do it again. And then again, and continue ad infinitum, to show that the area of the parabolic section is equal to
\begin{equation} \triangle ABC\left[1+\frac{1}{4}+\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^2+\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^3 + \cdots \right].\tag{20.1} \end{equation}
🔗
🔗
The sum in formula (20.1) is called a Geometric Series. You may have seen similar sums in the past. If so, and you know how to compute the value of this infinite sum, do that. You will find that it is equal to \(\frac{4}{3}\text{.}\) We will return to this question (how to compute the sum of a Geometric Series) in [provisional cross-reference: geometric series].
