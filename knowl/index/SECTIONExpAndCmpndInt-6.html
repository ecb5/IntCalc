Here’s why. If we invest \(\$1\) which pays nominal interest rate of \(5\%\) compounded annually then at the end of the first year our investment will pay us \(5\%\) of the amount we had invested during the year (\(\$1\) in this case) or \(\$0.05\text{.}\) This is added to the original investment of \(\$1\) for a total of \(\$1.05\text{.}\) During the second year we have \(\$1.05\) invested so at the end of the second year we’d receive \(5\%\) of \(\$1.05\) or \((\$1.05)\times(0.05) = 0.0525 \) which is added to the \((\$1.05)\) we started the year with for a total of
\begin{equation*} \underbrace{\left(1 + 0.05\right)}_{\text{first year}} + \underbrace{(0.05)\left(1 + 0.05\right)}_{\text{second year}} = \left(1 + 0.05\right)^2 = 1.1025 \text{ dollars}\text{.} \end{equation*}
Notice that the effective yield is slightly more than we would have received from simple interest. The nominal rate is the named rate of interest. It is the percentage which is used in the computations. You can think of it as the rate of interest earned at each moment of time.
