Dummy Variables:

The \(t\) in \(\dx{t}\) is what’s known as a dummy variable. In a very real sense \(t\) is not really there. It only appears to be. It is called a “dummy” because it is “dumb” in the old–fashioned sense of being mute. That is, when the differentials are all added up only \(x\) is left. There is no \(t\) on the right side of equation (3.5). Since the right and left sides are equal this means that there is no \(t\) on the left side either, despite appearances to the contrary.
