It was Joseph Fourier who first addressed this notational shortcoming by including these values in the integral symbol: \(\int_{t=a}^x\dx{t}\text{.}\) Since we know that the length of our line segment is the difference of the ending and starting coordinate we see that
\begin{gather} \int_{t=a}^x\dx{t} = x-a.\tag{20.4} \end{gather}
