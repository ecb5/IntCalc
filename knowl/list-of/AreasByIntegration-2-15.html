If you throw a dart randomly at a rectangle the probability of hitting the right half of the rectangle is \(\frac{1}{2}\text{,}\) of hitting the left \(\frac{1}{3}\) is \(\frac{1}{3}\text{,}\) of hitting the center \(\frac{2}{5}\) is \(\frac{2}{5}\) and so on. This idea is quite general and is called geometric probability. If you have a planar region \(R\text{,}\) with area \(A_1\) and a sub-region \(S\text{,}\) whose area is \(A_2\) the probabilty that a randomly chosen point in \(R\) will also be in the the sub-region is the ratio: \(\frac{A_1}{A_2}\text{.}\)
