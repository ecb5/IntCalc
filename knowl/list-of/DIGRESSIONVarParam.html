DIGRESSION: Variation of Parameters.

There are deep ideas hiding behind the “trick” of pretending that a constant from one solution is a function in another so it has been elevated to the status of “technique” and given the name Variation of Parameters. A detailed description of the technique is beyond the scope of this text. However, later in your education, you may well find yourself in a course called Ordinary Differential Equations where you will see it again. So we will introduce the terminology and outline the basics here.
🔗
When we write the differential equation from IVP (19.61) in the form
\begin{equation*} \dfdx{y}{y}-Ky=0 \end{equation*}
it is called a homogeneous equation (the right-hand side is zero). We’ve already written the tweaked (perturbed) equation as
\begin{equation*} \dfdx{y}{t} - Ky = - \left(1-\cos(t)\right) \end{equation*}
which is called a non-homogeneous equation (right-hand side is non-zero). The function on the right is called the forcing function.
🔗
As we’ve seen turns out to be successful in more than one context. Notice that it consists entirely of allowing the (constant) parameter \(A\) to vary (be variable).
