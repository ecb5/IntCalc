Definition 18.1.0.3. The Antiderivative and the Indefinite Integral.

Given a function \(f(x) \) the expression
\begin{equation*} \int f(x) \dx{x} \end{equation*}
represents the most general antiderivative of \(f(x)) \text{.}\) It is also sometimes called the indefinite integral of \(f(x) \text{.}\)
🔗
The term integral is generic. It can refer to a particular antiderivative, the most general antiderivative, the indefinite integral, or what we will be calling the definite integral in Chapter 20.
🔗
The function \(f(x)\) appearing between \(\int{}\) and \(\dx{x}\) is called the integrand of the integral.
