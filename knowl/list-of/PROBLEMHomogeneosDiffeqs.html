Problem 2.1.1.1. A Homogeneous Equation.

Consider the differential equation
\begin{equation} y^2\dfdx{y}{t}=2ty+y^2 \text{.}\tag{2.1} \end{equation}
As stated this is not a separable equation nor is there any other obvious way to find \(y(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
We will use the substitution \(y=ut\text{,}\) where \(u=u(t)\) is an unknown differentiable function, to turn this into a separable differential equation.
🔗
(a)
Show that if \(y=ut\) then \(\dfdx{y}{t} = u+t\dfdx{u}{t}\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
(b)
Use the substitution from part (a) to reduce equation (1.15) to
\begin{equation*} t\dfdx{u}{t} = u+u^2 \end{equation*}
which is separable.
🔗
🔗
(c)
Now separate the variables in the equation in part (b) and integrate to obtain
\begin{equation*} \int \frac{1}{u(u+1)}\dx{u} =\ln\abs{t}+C \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗
(d)
Show that
\begin{equation*} \frac{1}{u(u+1)}=\frac1u-\frac{1}{u+1} \end{equation*}
and use that to compute the integral in part (c).
🔗
🔗
(e)
Finally show that
\begin{equation*} y(t)=\frac{\kappa t^2}{1-\kappa t} \end{equation*}
where \(\kappa{}\) is an arbitrary constant.
🔗
🔗
It should be clear that the solution of a differential equation can be a tedious and painstaking process. Proceed slowly, check your work frequently, and be careful.
