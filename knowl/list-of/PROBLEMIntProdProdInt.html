Problem 19.3.0.18. When the Derivative of a Product is the Product of the Derivatives.

When faced with the integral of the product of two functions it is a common error to assume that we need only integrate the two factors separately and multiply the result. If we do that with formula (19.67) we’ll get
\begin{equation*} \frac{x^2}{2}\sin(x) +C\text{.} \end{equation*}
This is not correct as we can easily verify by differentiation. In fact we know that it is not generally true that
\begin{equation} \int f(x)\cdot g(x)\dx{x}=\left(\int f(x)\dx{x}\right)\cdot \left(\int g(x) \dx{x}\right)\tag{19.72} \end{equation}
because, we know that
\begin{equation*} \left(f\cdot g\right)^\prime(x) \neq f^\prime(x)\cdot g^\prime(x) \text{.} \end{equation*}
🔗
So when the integrand is the product of two functions we need a new idea.
🔗
Before we leave this topic behind entirely here is a curious fact. There are certain special pairs of functions for which it is true that
\begin{equation} \left(f\cdot g\right)^\prime(x) = f^\prime(x)\cdot g^\prime(x). \tag{19.73} \end{equation}
In this problem we will find a few examples of such pairs.
🔗

(a)

Suppose \(f^\prime(x)\neq{}f(x)\text{.}\) Show that if \(f(x)\) and \(g(x)\) satisfy equation (19.73) then
\begin{equation} g(x)=\exp\left(\int\frac{1}{1-\frac{f(x)}{f^\prime (x)}}\dx{x}\right)\text{.}\tag{19.74} \end{equation}
🔗
Why did we require that \(f^\prime(x)\neq{}f(x)\text{?}\) Are any other constraints on the functions needed?
🔗
🔗

(b)

Explain how you can tell that
\begin{equation} f(x)=\exp\left(\int\frac{1}{1-\frac{g(x)}{g^\prime (x)}}\dx{x}\right)\tag{19.75} \end{equation}
is also true.
🔗
