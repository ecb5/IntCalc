Integral Notation.

Leibniz suggested using the letter “S” (from the Latin word summa, meaning “total” or “sum”) to indicate such a sum. If Leibniz were inventing Calculus today the sum of the \(\dx{y}\)’s would be expressed this way:
\begin{gather*} S\dx{y}. \end{gather*}
But in Leibniz’ time the letter “S” looked like this: \(\int \text{.}\) So the statement that the sum of all the differentials of \(y\) is equal to \(y\) has come down to us as:
\begin{gather} y=\int\dx{y}.\tag{18.4} \end{gather}
