Problem 19.1.5.5.

We have the following lines from Table 18.1.0.4:
\begin{align*} \dx(\inverse\sin(x))= \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\dx{x} \amp{}\amp{} \int\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\dx{x}=\inverse\sin(x)+C \end{align*}
\begin{align*} \dx(\inverse\tan(x))= \frac{1}{1+x^2}\dx{x} \amp{}\amp{} \int\frac{1}{1+x^2}\dx{x}=\inverse\tan(x)+C \end{align*}
\begin{align*} \dx(\inverse\sec(x))= \frac{1}{x\sqrt{x^2-1}}\dx{x} \amp{}\amp{} \int\frac{1}{x\sqrt{x^2-1}}\dx{x}=\inverse\sec(x)+C \end{align*}
Let’s pretend for a moment that you’ve forgotten these lines (Though we know you would never do that.). Use the appropriate trigonometric substitution to obtain those three integrals on the right-hand side.
