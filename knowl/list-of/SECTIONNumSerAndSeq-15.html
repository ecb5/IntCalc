Example 2.2.0.5.

Suppose \(b\) is a fixed real number with \(\left|b\right|\lt{}1\text{.}\) Use the definition to show that \({\mathop{\mathrm{lim}}_{n\to \infty } b^n}=0\text{.}\)
🔗

Scrapwork 2.2.0.1.

We will be starting with \(\epsilon >0\) and we wish to find a real number \(B\) so that
\begin{equation*} \left|b^n\right|\lt{}\epsilon \end{equation*}
whenever \(n>B\text{.}\) As is often the case, we will work backwards to determine what \(B\) will work. With that in mind, we want
🔗
🔗
\begin{equation*} \left|b^n\right|={\left|b\right|}^n\lt{}\epsilon \end{equation*}
If \(b\neq 0\) then we can take the natural logarithm of both sides to obtain.
🔗
\begin{equation*} {\ln \left({\left|b\right|}^n\right)}\lt{}{\ln( \eps) } \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} n{\ln\abs{ b} }\lt{}{\ln( \eps )n } \end{equation*}
