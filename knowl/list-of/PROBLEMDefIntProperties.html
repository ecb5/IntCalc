Problem 20.3.0.3. Properties of the Definite Integral.

This problem is very illuminative and will help you develop your intuition about definite integrals. You should do it—or at least give it serious though—whether your instructor has assigned it or not.
🔗
Give an example of a finite sum which displays each of the following properties of the definite integral, and show that each property is true for finite sums.
🔗

(a) Property 1.

\(\displaystyle \int_{x=a}^b0\dx{x}=0\)
🔗
🔗

(b) Property 2.

\(\displaystyle \int_{x=a}^af(x)\dx{x}=0\)
🔗
🔗

(c) Property 3.

\(\displaystyle \int_{x=a}^bcf(x)\dx{x}=c\int_{x=a}^bf(x)\dx{x}\)
🔗
🔗

(d) Property 4.

\(\displaystyle \int_{x=a}^bf(x)\dx{x}=-\int_{x=b}^af(x)\dx{x}\)
🔗
Hint.
Integrating from \(a\) to \(b\) accumulates the sum in one direction, so that \(b-a\) is the (finite) difference \(\Delta x\text{.}\) Integrating from \(b\) to \(a\) accumulates the sum in the other, so \(a-b\) is the (finite) difference \(\Delta x\) .
🔗
🔗
🔗

(e) Property 5.

\(\displaystyle \int_{x=a}^bf(x)+g(x) \dx{x} =\int_{x=a}^bf(x)\dx{x} + \int_{x=a}^bg(x)\dx{x}\)
🔗
🔗

(f) Property 6.

\(\displaystyle \int_{x=a}^bf(x)\dx{x}{}=\int_{x=a}^cf(x)\dx{x}+\int_{x=c}^bf(x)\dx{x}\)
