Problem 19.1.3.6.

Notice that in Table 18.1.0.4 we have the entries
\begin{align*} \int\sin (x)\dx{x}=-\cos \left(x\right)+C\amp{}\amp{}\text{and}\amp{}\amp{} \int\cos (x)\dx{x}=\sin (x)+C \end{align*}
But we don’t have an entry for\(\int\tan (x)\dx{x}\) or for \(\int\sec\left(x\right)\dx{x}\text{.}\) Substitution allows us to fill in these gaps.
🔗

(a)

Use an appropriate substitution to show
\begin{equation*} \int\tan (x)\dx{x}=-\ln \abs{\cos \left(x\right)}+C. \end{equation*}
🔗
Hint.
\(\tan (x)=\frac{\sin \left(x\right)}{\cos \left(x\right)}\)
🔗
🔗
🔗

(b)

Apply a similar technique to compute \(\int\cot (x) \dx{x}\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

(c)

Since \(\tan(x)=\frac{\sec(x)\tan(x)}{\sec(x)}\) we could also use the substitution \({u=\sec\left(x\right)}\) to compute \(\int\tan\left(x\right)\dx{x}\text{.}\) Do we get the same answer? Explain.
🔗
🔗
If we try a similar approach for the integral \(\int\sec\left(x\right)\dx{x}=\int\frac{1}{\cos\left(x\right)}\dx{x}\text{,}\) we will hit a dead end. Try it and see. We will develop a more general approach to integrals like these in Cornerstone Problem 19.1.3.8.
