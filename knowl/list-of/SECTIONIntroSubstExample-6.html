Example 19.1.1.4.

Now suppose we “complicate” [cross-reference to target(s) "EXAMPLESimpleDiffeq" missing or not unique] a bit. Suppose we want to solve the differential equation
\begin{equation} \dfdx{y}{x}=(1+x)(1+y)\text{.}\tag{19.3} \end{equation}
🔗
Separating variables as before we obtain
\begin{equation*} \int{\frac{1}{1+y}\dx{y}}=\int{\left(1+x\right)\dx{x}} \end{equation*}
The integral on the right is the same as before, but the one on the left does not appear in Table 18.1.0.4 so we have to be clever.
🔗
If we make the substitution \(z=1+y\text{,}\) then \(\dx{z}=\dx{y}\) and the above equation is transformed into
\begin{equation} \int{\frac{1}{z}\dx{z}}=\int{\left(1+x\right)\dx{x}}\tag{19.4} \end{equation}
which we can solve.
