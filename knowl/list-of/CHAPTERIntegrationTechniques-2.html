As we mentioned in the previous chapter, a powerful use of Calculus is to create differential equations to model phenomena. The computation of integrals is one a tool for the solution of those equations. As differential equations become more complex, we need to expand our integration techniques to account for more complicated integrands. As a starting point, we will reintroduce the powerful notion of a substitution to make a problem easier of the eyes.
🔗
For example, entry #7 of Table 18.1.0.4 tells us that, \(\int\cos(x){\dx{x} = \sin(x)+C }\text{.}\) But with a little cleverness we can use it to evaluate other integrals as well. “Cleverness” comes in many forms but as we’ve seen one of the simplest ways to be clever is to make our formulas “easier on the eyes” with a substitution.
🔗
