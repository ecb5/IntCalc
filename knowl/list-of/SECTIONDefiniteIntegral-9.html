Properties of the Definite Integral.

\begin{align} \text{\bf{} Property 1:}\amp{} \int_{x=a}^b0\dx{x}=0\tag{20.7}\\ \text{\bf{} Property 2:}\amp{} \int_{x=a}^af(x)\dx{x}=0\tag{20.8}\\ \text{\bf{} Property 3:}\amp{} \int_{x=a}^bcf(x)\dx{x}=c\int_{x=a}^bf(x)\dx{x}\tag{20.9}\\ \text{\bf{} Property 4:}\amp{} \int_{x=a}^bf(x)\dx{x}=-\int_{x=b}^af(x)\dx{x}\tag{20.10}\\ \text{\bf{} Property 5:}\amp{} \int_{x=a}^bf(x)+g(x) \dx{x} =\int_{x=a}^bf(x)\dx{x} + \int_{x=a}^bg(x)\dx{x}\tag{20.11}\\ \text{\bf{} Property 6:}\amp{} \int_{x=a}^bf(x)\dx{x}{}=\int_{x=a}^cf(x)\dx{x}+\int_{x=c}^bf(x)\dx{x}\tag{20.12} \end{align}

Problem 20.3.0.3.

Give an example of a finite sum which displays each of the properties of the definite integral, and show that each property is true for finite sums.
🔗
Hint. For Property 4
Integrating from \(a\) to \(b\) accumulates the sum in one direction, so that \(b-a\) is the (finite) difference \(\Delta x\text{.}\) Integrating from \(b\) to \(a\) accumulates the sum in the other, so \(a-b\) is the (finite) difference \(\Delta x\) .
