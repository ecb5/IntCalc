Example 13.2.0.1. Separating the Variables of a Simple Differential Equation.

To see this in the context of a differential equation, consider
\begin{equation} \dfdx{y}{x} =xy\text{.}\tag{13.6} \end{equation}
Before we continue see if you can come up with a solution of equation (13.6) on your own.
🔗
Separating the variables we have
\begin{equation*} \frac{\dx{y}}{y}=x\dx{x}\text{.} \end{equation*}
Integrating both side gives
\begin{equation} \ln\abs{y} +C_1= \frac{x^2}{2}+C_2\tag{13.7} \end{equation}
where \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) are the integration constants arising from the integrations.
