Problem 20.1.1.4.

To see that the area of triangle \(ABC\) is four times that of triangle \(ABF\text{,}\) first note that the area of triangle \(ABC\) is four times the area of triangle \(DBE\) (why?) and that the area of triangle \(BFD\) is the same as the area of triangle \(DFA\) as they have the same base and equal heights. It is left to show that triangle \(BEF\) has the same area as those two triangles. To see this, we will put in a coordinate system.
🔗

(a)

Show that the coordinates of \(D\) are \(\left(\frac{a+b}{2},\frac{a^2+b^2}{2}\right)\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

(b)

Show that the coordinates of \(F\) are \(\left(\frac{a+b}{2},{\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)}^2\right)\)
🔗
🔗

(c)

Show that the coordinates of \(E\) are \(\left(\frac{a+b}{2},ab\right)\)
🔗
Hint.
Recall that \(BC\) is tangent to the parabola at \(B\text{.}\)
