Example 8.7.0.1. Population Growth.

Suppose we start with a colony of \(10\) grams of bacteria in a Petri dish and we wish to model the growth of the population as a function of time.
🔗
In light of what we said before, let’s assume that this bacteria grows at a continuous rate which is proportional to its size. While it is true that the bacteria reproduce in a discrete manner, it is not unreasonable to assume this over a short period. With that in mind, let \(P(t)\) denote the size of the bacteria culture in grams at time \(t\) in days and let’s assume that the nominal growth rate is \(30\%\text{.}\)
🔗
Since we started with \(10\) grams of bacteria we have the initial condition \(P(0)=10\text{.}\) This says that the amount of bacteria at time \(t\) must satisfy the IVP:
\begin{align} \dfdx{P}{t}= 0.3P,\amp{}\amp{} P(0)=10.\tag{8.19} \end{align}
Take specific notice that the differential equation in IVP (8.19) expresses the idea that the rate of change of the population, \(\dfdx{P}{t}\text{,}\) is proportional to the size of the population, \(P\text{,}\) and that the constant of proportionality is \(0.3\text{,}\) or \(30\%\text{.}\)
🔗
Clearly there is nothing particularly special about the number \(0.3.\) If our colony had been increasing at a nominal rate of \(15\%\) we’d have arrived at the IVP
\begin{align*} \dfdx{P}{t}=0.15P,\amp{}\amp{} P(0)=10. \end{align*}
